# Survey

10 to 12 questions, under three minutes. Circulated to reach beyond the interview sample.

**Header text:**

> This survey is for a university course project studying how students use the FAST student portal.
> It takes about three minutes. Your answers are anonymous, we do not ask for your name or roll
> number, and you can stop at any point.

---

## Screening

1. Which year are you in? *(1st / 2nd / 3rd / 4th)*
2. Which device do you use most often to check the portal? *(Phone / Laptop / Shared or borrowed device / Computer lab)*

## Behaviour (A1)

3. In a normal week, how many times do you open the portal? *(0-2 / 3-5 / 6-10 / More than 10)*
4. Do you track your attendance anywhere other than the portal? *(No / Yes, in my head / Yes, in notes or a spreadsheet / Yes, a friend tracks it for the group)*
5. If yes, has your own record ever turned out to be wrong? *(Never / Once / More than once / I don't check it against the portal)*
6. Where do you usually first hear that marks have been posted? *(The portal / A classmate / A group chat / The instructor)*

## Anxiety (A2)

7. Rank these by how much they worry you, most first: *(attendance dropping · marks published · fee due · registration opening)*
8. Have you ever found out about one of the above too late to act on it? *(No / Yes, attendance / Yes, a fee / Yes, registration / Yes, more than one)*

## Comprehension

9. On the grade report, do you know what the coloured cells and single-letter codes mean? *(Yes, all of them / Some / No / I have never looked at that screen)*
10. Which of these portal terms could you define right now? *(select all: MCA · CLO · PLO · Cr. Ernd · ARN · none of these)*

## Notifications (A3)

11. If the portal could send you alerts, which would you turn on? *(select all, plus "none")*
12. Would getting alerts about your academic standing make you feel more in control, more stressed, or no different?

## Optional

13. What is the single most annoying thing about the portal? *(free text)*

---

**Reporting rule:** report counts and proportions out of the actual response total. Do not round a
sample of 23 into a percentage that implies a survey of hundreds, and state the sample size next to
every figure that reaches the final report.
