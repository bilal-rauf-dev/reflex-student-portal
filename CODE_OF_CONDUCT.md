# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be someone the rest of the group is glad to work with.

## Our standards

**Expected:**

- Review each other's work honestly. A rubber-stamp approval is not a kindness.
- Disagree with the idea, not the person. Say what you would do instead.
- Ask when you are stuck. A sixteen-week schedule has no room for struggling quietly.
- Do what you said you would do, and say early when you cannot.
- Credit people's work accurately, in commits and in the viva.

**Not acceptable:**

- Harassment, personal attacks, or discriminatory language of any kind.
- Publishing anyone's personal information, including any real student's records.
- Taking credit for work you did not do, or letting a teammate carry your section.
- Dismissing accessibility concerns as optional polish. On this project they are the point.

## Research participants

Phase 2 and Phase 5 involve real students giving us their time. Treat them accordingly: explain what
the study is for before they agree, let them stop at any moment without a reason, anonymise responses
at the point of collection, and never discuss an identifiable participant's performance outside the
group.

A participant struggling with the prototype is data about our design, never a joke about them.

## Scope

This applies to the repository, the group's channels, project meetings, testing sessions and any
public presentation of the work.

## Enforcement

Raise concerns with the group lead, **24F-0508 Bilal Rauf**. If the concern is about the lead, raise it
with the course instructor. Serious or repeated conduct issues in a group project belong with the
instructor, since they affect both the work and the individual assessment.
